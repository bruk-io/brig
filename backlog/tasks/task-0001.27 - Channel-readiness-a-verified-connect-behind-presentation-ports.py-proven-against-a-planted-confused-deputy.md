## Acceptance Criteria
<!-- AC:BEGIN -->
- [ ] #1 The public API waits for a channel to be ready up to a deadline and reports ready, not ready, or a typed refusal when the process that called listen() on the answering socket is not in the jail's PID namespace (decision-0005: it proves a jail process called listen(), not that it is the one accepting); the check is the one TASK-0001.24 recorded and decision-0005 decided, reached through a port in presentation/ports.py with a fake for integration tests
- [ ] #2 An e2e battery on real bwrap plants the confused deputy (the reserved path symlinked to a listener outside the jail) and readiness refuses it; its positive control, the jail's own listener at the same path, reads ready; a path that exists with nothing listening reads not ready
- [ ] #3 The LikeC4 model shows infra/sockets and the readiness relationship, and likec4 validate and c4lint are clean
- [ ] #4 Every OSError from the peer lookup (SO_PEERPIDFD, the ns/pid readlink, the pidfd re-check), including EINVAL from SO_PEERPIDFD on a reaped peer (measured), is a refusal value: never raised out of the readiness primitive and never a fallback to the bare SO_PEERCRED pid; a test pins it
- [ ] #5 On a kernel without SO_PEERPIDFD (Linux before 6.5), readiness refuses (fails closed) with a typed reason and never falls back to SO_PEERCRED alone; a test pins it
<!-- AC:END -->

finish-moved (2026-10-19): AC#1 reworded and AC#4/AC#5 added after acceptance. Why: TASK-0001.24's attack skeptic measured that SO_PEERCRED/SO_PEERPIDFD name the listen() caller, not the acceptor (cases C and C2, both READY); both skeptics measured that SO_PEERPIDFD raises EINVAL on a reaped peer (the spike's probe let it escape); and the attack skeptic reasoned, without measuring it, that the SO_PEERCRED-only fallback on kernels before 6.5 reopens that pid-reuse window. The owner's threat-model ruling (@bruk, 2026-10-19) put same-uid host processes inside the jail's trust boundary, so readiness means a process in the jail's PID namespace called listen() on the socket that answered. All recorded in decision-0005.
